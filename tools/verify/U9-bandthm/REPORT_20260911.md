## Exact counterexamples

**P0 and the domain of P5.5.** With two goods, two agents, `M=(1,1)`, all utilities
one, and `delta=1`, assigning both goods to agent 1 is band-feasible. Its gains
are `(2,0)`. Thus `G(1)` is not a subset of the strictly positive orthant. This
does not refute refined Slater or positive optimal gains: equal sharing is a
positive feasible point, and zero gains have log objective minus infinity.

For the OA loop take two goods, three agents, `M=(1,1)`, `delta=1`, and utilities
`u=((1,1/3),(1/3,1),(2/3,2/3))`. Start from the usual uniform allocation, whose
gains are `(4/9,4/9,4/9)`. The first good's tangent coefficients are
`(9/4,3/4,3/2)`; the second's are `(3/4,9/4,3/2)`. The unique master optimum gives
good 1 to agent 1 and good 2 to agent 2. Its masses `(1,1,0)` satisfy the band
`[0,4/3]`, but its gains are `(1,1,0)`. The next tangent at zero is undefined.
Even retaining the initial incumbent cannot terminate at tolerance `1/100`: the
exact optimal gains are `(2/3,2/3,4/9)`, certified by KKT in the artifact, and the
initial objective gap is `log(9/4)>1/100`. First-master safety remains intact.
The original proof invokes the positive lower bound from P5.3, which only applies
when `delta<1`. That restriction, or an explicit positive-gain safeguard, is
missing from P5.5's statement. The delivered grid ending at `0.33` avoids this case.

**P4.5, with a positive release point.** Let `k=n=2`, `M=(1,1)`, `rho=0`, and
use utilities by agent

```text
u = ((1, 1/3), (1/2, 1/3)).
```

All utilities exceed `lambda*M` with the model's `lambda=0.30`. For
`0 <= d <= 1/6`, a globally optimal assignment and its gains are

```text
X(d) = ((1-d, 0), (d, 1)),    g(d) = (1-d, (2+3d)/6).
s(d) = (1-6d)/((1-d)(2+3d)),  nu = (-s(d), 0),
p = (3/(2+3d), 2/(2+3d)).
```

The band masses are `(1-d,1+d)`. All occupied entries satisfy stationarity
exactly; the only unused entry has residual
`(1+9d)/(3(1-d)(2+3d))>0`. Multipliers have the required signs and complementary
slackness. These facts are symbolic in `d`, so concave KKT sufficiency establishes
global optimality throughout the interval, rather than only at sampled values.
At `d=1/6`, `nu=0`, proving that this allocation is also unconstrained optimal.
It stays feasible for every wider band. Therefore

```text
phi(d) = log((1-d)(2+3d)/6),  0 <= d <= 1/6;
phi(d) = log(25/72),         d >= 1/6.
```

The derivative is `s(d)>0` before `delta_c=1/6`, but tends to zero there.
Both one-sided derivatives are zero. This directly refutes the assertion that
being nonconstant to the left forces a positive left derivative at the plateau.
Interior supergradient and one-sided derivative formulas remain valid. At the
domain endpoint zero, only a right derivative exists; the interior interval
formula must not be used as an ordinary two-sided derivative statement there.

**P4.8, with an actual integral delivered allocation.** In the same fixture,
deliver good 1 to agent 1 and good 2 to agent 2. Then `delta_0=0`, `V=log(1/3)`.
At the claimed tier 2 threshold, the set in P4.8 is exactly `(alpha,infinity)`,
where

```text
alpha = (1-sqrt(25-24*exp(1/200)))/6 = 0.01034617151377977...
```

Here `0<alpha<1/6`: `1<exp(1/200)<200/199<25/24`. The objective gap equals the
threshold at `alpha`, exceeds it above `alpha`, and is smaller below it.
Consequently the set has infimum `alpha` and no minimum. Bisection with a bracket
can approximate the infimum; monotonicity does not create a minimum of a strict
superlevel set. The empty-set case also needs a convention.

## Why the retained claims survive

P0: `X=1/k` is feasible and has positive gains. All constraints are affine, so
refined Slater holds, even with coincident band bounds. The allocation polytope
is compact. Interpreting zero gains as log value minus infinity makes the
objective upper semicontinuous there; a finite optimum is attained with positive
gains. Strict concavity of the sum of logs gives unique optimal gains by the
midpoint argument. Neither fact requires every feasible gain vector positive.

P1: embed an integral district assignment as its incidence matrix. The supply
and band masses agree exactly. The fractional objective at that point minus the
delivered objective is `rho*(C-Chat)>=0` under H3. Maximization gives the bound.
An integral infeasible point cannot be compared by this embedding, and an empty
integral feasible set makes the bound vacuous. Exact enumeration supplements
this general argument in the new artifact.

P2: differentiate the log Lagrangian to get `u/g <= p+nu*M`, with equality on
support. Multiply by `x` and sum to get unit personalized expenditure and
anonymous expenditure `1-nu*m`. Positivity follows from `u/g>0`. For `delta>0`,
some agent has mass at most the target, hence zero upper-band multiplier and
`nu<=0`; its price inequalities force every `p_z>0`. A nonzero `nu` makes
anonymous unit-budget demand fail. The good-side additive score follows by
rearranging stationarity. The original ratio rule fails for a shared single
good valued `(1,1/2)`, whose equal-share optimal gains are `(1/2,1/4)`.
The symbolic gauge calculation preserves `q`; sign and complementarity still
restrict the allowed gauge except at `delta=0`.

P3: on support, the normalized budget-row coefficients equal `p_z+nu_i*M_z`.
Thus the weighted sum of supply rows and tight-agent mass rows minus all budget
rows is identically zero. Its budget coefficients are `-1`, so this dependency
is nontrivial even when every `nu_i=0`. An extreme point has independent positive
columns, giving `|support|<=n+k+t-1`, hence at most `k-1+t` split goods. Coincident
upper/lower rows count once. The artifact checks exact ranks, including `k=1`,
all-tight and all-slack examples. Conversely, four identical goods shared equally
by two agents give four splits at a nonvertex, exceeding the coarse bound three.

P4's retained parts: feasible sets are nested. Mixing feasible assignments at
two widths gives feasibility at the mixed width; Jensen proves value concavity.
The log tangent inequality and KKT, applied to any other feasible assignment,
give `phi(d')<=phi(d)+(T/k)*sum(mu_plus+mu_minus)*(d'-d)`. This proves the
supergradient directly. Adding the same nonnegative constant to both multipliers
at zero width preserves the dual objective and makes the aggregate unbounded.
Minimization must range over the exact dual-optimal set, including gauge choices.
Subtracting the delivered value gives softness; taking minimum supporting lines
and interpolating chords gives the two-sided envelope. No obligatory kink is used.

P5 safety: `tangent_h(g)-log(g)` has second derivative `1/g^2>0` and its unique
minimum is zero at `g=h`. Every positive-cut master therefore majorizes the log
objective. More cuts shrink its feasible region. Cuts at each optimal gain make
the summed tangent exact by first-order concave optimality. Valid dual bounds
remain bounds even after a time limit or with a nonzero solver gap; the stopped
primal incumbent is the unsafe quantity. The new LP formulation checks 16 optimal
masters against the analytic solution, with primal/dual residuals at tier 1.

For the corrected positive compact domain `0<=delta<1`, the model's epsilon
convergence argument closes: all gains have a uniform lower bound `l>0`, so the
log gradient is Lipschitz. If infinitely many iterates had model error at least
epsilon, compactness would give two arbitrarily close iterates, with the older
one's tangent already present at the newer iteration. Taylor's bound makes that
tangent error at most `||g_new-g_old||^2/(2*l^2)`, a contradiction. This is not a
claim of exact finite termination. The historical finite 250-iteration experiment
alone cannot prove nontermination of an infinite sequence.

P6: each score is a linear functional of `(u_1(z),...,u_k(z),M_z)`. Its maximum
equals `p_z`, with equality for every assigned agent. Evaluating these scores and
the primal/KKT conditions takes `O(nk)` operations. This gives utility-space cells
and a certificate, not geographic cells or contiguity.

## Citation and environment limits

[Jalota et al., Fisher Markets with Linear Constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10412)
and [Echenique et al., Constrained Pseudo-market Equilibrium](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05986).
These sources distinguish constraint prices and budget adjustments from ordinary
equal-budget demand. The mathematical claims above use the explicit KKT proof.

with EF1 plus fPO established for personalized bivalued valuations or at most two
valuation types. That theorem does not establish the weighted opportunity-band
claim here. Echenique's sufficient fairness condition is not an impossibility
theorem when its hypothesis fails. No new fairness guarantee is certified here.
