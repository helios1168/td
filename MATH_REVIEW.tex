\begin{abstract}
This review presents an exhaustive mathematical and axiomatic appraisal of the multi-channel sales territory design and wholesaler staffing optimization architecture documented across \texttt{docs/MODEL\_FULL.md}, the operational solvers, and the latest production pipelines. We analyze the historical decomposition spanning state-level mixed-integer linear programming (Level~0), semi-discrete optimal transport with discrete graph repair heuristics (Level~2), and Nash social welfare bipartite matching (Stage~2), detailing the mathematical root causes that produced combinatorial timeouts and heuristic boundary slacks. 

To overcome these structural limitations, we formulate and evaluate the newly implemented \textbf{Support-Based Exact Reformulation Framework}. By pre-enumerating connected graph supports of bounded spatial diameter ($\le 900$~km) on the planning graph and solving an exact binary Master MILP over continuous contact shares, this paradigm guarantees 100\% rook contiguity, exact capacity band satisfaction, and zero unheld opportunity mass by construction, solving to provable global optimality in under 3 seconds. Furthermore, we examine the \textbf{Unified Multi-Channel Architecture}, which resolves the sparse geography paradox across 7 rural Western states through a dedicated regional merged channel (WIFI/WHFI), realizing 36 certified Pareto-optimal scenarios ($K \in [46, 53]$ total districts).

On the axiomatic front, we prove the exact scale invariance of Nash Social Welfare under multiplicative descaling and demonstrate why multiplicative normalization fails to alter the dimensionless curvature of the welfare function. We show that while a zero disagreement point ($d=\mathbf{0}$) historically anchored the classical Envy-Free up to One Item (EF1) guarantee of Caragiannis et al.~(2019), that guarantee is already invalidated by hard capacity bands and spatial contiguity. We formalize \textbf{Feasible Envy-Freeness (FEFx)} under Generalized Assignment Constraints (Barman et al., 2023) paired with asymmetric claims-scaled reservation floors ($d_i = \alpha_i S_i + d_{\text{floor}}$), restoring steep concavity to protect incumbent wholesaler books while guaranteeing bargaining set non-emptiness. Finally, we document the planar polygon dissolution pipeline and the complete 33,791-ZCTA nationwide crosswalk ($6,082,380$ rows) that eliminates spatial omissions in downstream enterprise reporting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}

The commercial sales territory design and staffing problem formulated in \texttt{docs/MODEL\_FULL.md} addresses the simultaneous partitioning of nationwide sales opportunity across the 48 contiguous United States and Washington, D.C. ($S=49$ planning states) into $K$ balanced sales districts. Concurrently, it models the assignment of incumbent sales representatives across four fine sales channels:
\begin{equation}
C = \{N_{\text{WH}}, N_{\text{FI}}, \text{WH}, \text{FI}\}
\end{equation}
admitting seven permissible channel bundle configurations:
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak{B} = \{N, \text{WH}, \text{FI}, \text{WH\_PLUS}, \text{FI\_PLUS}, \text{WHFI}, \text{WHFI\_PLUS}\}
\end{equation}
where $N = \{N_{\text{WH}}, N_{\text{FI}}\}$, $\text{WH} = \{\text{WH}\}$, $\text{FI} = \{\text{FI}\}$, and $\text{WHFI} = \{\text{WH}, \text{FI}\}$.

\subsection{Monolithic MINLP Formulation}
Formulated as a monolithic optimization problem over the set of discrete geographic units $Z$ ($|Z| = 6,459$ commercial ZIP codes, or $|Z| = 33,791$ total Census ZCTAs), the mathematical program is an intractable Mixed-Integer Non-Convex Non-Linear Program (MINLP):
\begin{align}
    \max_{x, y, w} \quad & \sum_{i \in R} \log\left( g_i(x, y, w_i) \right) - \rho \sum_{j=1}^K \text{Perimeter}(A_j) \label{eq:minlp_obj} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{j=1}^K x_{z, j, c} = 1 && \forall z \in Z, c \in C \label{eq:minlp_part} \\
    & L_{B_j} \le \sum_{z \in Z} \sum_{c \in B_j} M_{z,c} x_{z,j,c} \le U_{B_j} && \forall j \in \{1, \dots, K\} \label{eq:minlp_cap} \\
    & G[A_j] \text{ is a connected planar subgraph} && \forall j \in \{1, \dots, K\} \label{eq:minlp_contig} \\
    & \sum_{i \in R} w_{i,j} = 1, \quad \sum_{j=1}^K w_{i,j} \le 1 && \forall j, i \label{eq:minlp_match} \\
    & x_{z,j,c} \in \{0, 1\}, \quad w_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\} && \forall z, j, c, i \label{eq:minlp_bin}
\end{align}
The combination of NP-hard planar graph partitioning, non-linear logarithmic Nash social welfare $\sum \log g_i$, channel bundling product-form constraints, and quadratic perimeter penalties renders direct global optimization across 6,459 geographic units computationally intractable.

To achieve tractability, the operational architecture initially adopted a \textbf{three-level hierarchical decomposition}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Level 0 (Macro Planning):} A state-by-channel MILP solved sequentially on the state rook graph $G_S = (S, E_S)$.
    \item \textbf{Level 2 (Micro Realization):} A semi-discrete optimal transport LP per split state, followed by discrete graph repair heuristics on the Voronoi dual graph $G_{\text{cell}}$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Wholesaler Staffing):} A maximum-weight bipartite matching on logarithmic gains solved via the Kuhn-Munkres (Hungarian) algorithm.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Legacy Decomposition and Its Structural Failure Modes}

\subsection{Level 0: Macro-Level Channel Planning (State \texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x} Channel MILP)}
Level~0 abstracts geography to the state grain. For each slot $j \in J$ belonging to bundle $B_j$, the decision space comprises:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item $y_{s,j} \in [0, 1]$: Continuous share of state $s$ allocated to slot $j$.
    \item $z_{s,j} \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary contact indicator ($y_{s,j} > 0$).
    \item $u_j \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary slot utilization indicator.
    \item $r_{s,j} \in \{0, 1\}$: Contiguity tree root indicator.
    \item $f_{a,j} \in [0, N_S-1]$: Commodity flow on directed arc $a \in A_S$ where $N_S = |S| = 49$.
\end{itemize}

The core relations governing Level~0 are:
\begin{align}
    \text{Product-form Coverage:} \quad & \sum_{j : c \in B_j} y_{s,j} \le 1 - p_{s,c} && \forall s \in S, c \in C \label{eq:cover} \\
    \text{Contact Sizing:} \quad & \eta z_{s,j} \le y_{s,j} \le z_{s,j} \le u_j && \forall s \in S, j \in J \label{eq:contact} \\
    \text{Capacity Bands:} \quad & L_{B_j} u_j \le \sum_{s \in S} W_{s,j} y_{s,j} \le U_{B_j} u_j + \sum_{s \text{ capped}} a_s z_{s,j} && \forall j \in J \label{eq:band} \\
    \text{Tree Contiguity:} \quad & \sum_{s \in S} r_{s,j} = u_j, \quad r_{s,j} \le z_{s,j} && \forall j \in J \label{eq:root} \\
    & f_{a,j} \le (N_S-1) z_{\text{tail}(a), j}, \quad f_{a,j} \le (N_S-1) z_{\text{head}(a), j} && \forall a \in A_S, j \in J \label{eq:flow_cap} \\
    & z_{s,j} - N_S r_{s,j} \le \sum_{a \in \delta^-(s)} f_{a,j} - \sum_{a \in \delta^+(s)} f_{a,j} && \forall s \in S, j \in J \label{eq:flow_net}
\end{align}
where $W_{s,j} = \sum_{c \in B_j} M_{s,c}$, $p_{s,c}$ represents prior committed coverage, and $a_s = \max(0, W_s / \text{cap}_s - \tau_B)$ provides state-specific band-break allowances under split caps $\text{cap}_s$.

\subsection{Level 2: Micro-Level Realization (Optimal Transport and Graph Repair)}
Given state shares $y_{s,j}$, unsplit states are mapped entirely to district $j$. Split states are partitioned via the semi-discrete optimal transport LP:
\begin{align}
    \min_{x} \quad & \sum_{z \in s} \sum_{j} M_z \left( \|p_z - c_j\|^2 + \pi_{z,j} \right) x_{z,j} \label{eq:ot_obj} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{j} x_{z,j} = 1 \quad \forall z \in s, \qquad \sum_{z \in s} M_z x_{z,j} = y_{s,j} M_s \quad \forall j \label{eq:ot_cons}
\end{align}
Centroids $c_j$ are iteratively shifted via up to five Lloyd updates. Resulting disconnected components are reconnected post-LP via heuristic frontier expansion and bridging on the Voronoi dual graph $G_{\text{cell}}$ with capacity slack $\sigma = 0.02 \tau_B$.

\subsection{Structural Breakdown and Failure Modes of the Legacy Pipeline}
Rigorous mathematical auditing identified four critical vulnerabilities in this decomposition:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Combinatorial Explosion in Multi-Channel MILPs:} When moving from single-channel National (14 districts) to multi-channel execution (WH with 11--14 districts and FI with 21--26 districts), the binary flow-tree formulation (\ref{eq:root})--(\ref{eq:flow_net}) suffered catastrophic branch-and-bound growth. Solvers frequently exceeded 600-second timeouts without closing optimality gaps.
    \item \textbf{Topological Disconnection under Convex Power Cells:} By the Brenier-Aurenhammer theorem of optimal transport, the continuous LP solutions partition Euclidean space into convex polyhedral power diagrams. When intersecting convex power cells with non-convex administrative geographies (coastal inlets, peninsulas, river boundaries, panhandles), discrete ZIP code assignments frequently fragmented into disconnected topological islands.
    \item \textbf{Heuristic Slippage and Unheld Mass:} The post-hoc discrete repair heuristics on $G_{\text{cell}}$ lacked global guarantees. To bridge disconnected islands across state lines, the heuristic permitted $\pm 2\%$ capacity slacks, frequently causing unheld mass ($>0.5\%$) or subtle rook-contiguity violations in multi-county corridors.
    \item \textbf{Timing Inversion in Stage 2 Matching:} In the legacy sequence, staffing matching was executed at Level~0 based on expected continuous state shares $y_{s,j}$, before Level~2 determined the actual physical boundaries. Because sales books $S_i(z)$ are clustered discrete point masses rather than continuous fluids, matching on continuous state shares produced substantial book displacement once realized boundaries were finalized.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Support-Based Exact Reformulation Framework}

To resolve the failure modes of the legacy pipeline, we developed and deployed the \textbf{Support-Based Exact Reformulation Framework}. This formulation replaces both the flow-based Level~0 MILP and the heuristic Level~2 repair with an exact, support-enumerated mathematical program.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}
Let $G = (V, E)$ denote the planning graph (where $V$ represents states or sub-state macro units such as Northern California and Southern California, with $|V| = 50$). 

\begin{definition}[Admissible Support Space]\label{def:support}
A subset of vertices $S \subseteq V$ is an \textbf{admissible support} ($S \in \mathcal{S}$) if and only if it satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Induced Subgraph Contiguity:} The induced subgraph $G[S] = (S, E[S])$ is connected.
    \item \textbf{Cardinality Bound:} $|S| \le \bar{s}$, where $\bar{s} \in \{5, 6\}$ limits administrative sprawl.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Diameter Ceiling:} The maximum pairwise geographic centroid distance satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
        \max_{u, v \in S} \|c_u - c_v\|_2 \le D_{\max} \label{eq:diam}
    \end{equation}
    where $D_{\max} = 900$~km for standard CONUS supports, with an expanded ceiling of $1,200$~km for sparse Pacific Northwest supports (e.g., WA, OR, ID).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

By pre-computing $\mathcal{S}$, contiguity is enforced \textbf{a priori by construction}. Graph cycle checks and flow variables are completely eliminated from the optimization problem.

\subsection{The Master Support Partitioning MILP}
Let $K$ denote the exact required number of districts. For each admissible support $S \in \mathcal{S}$, let $x_S \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the binary support activation indicator, and for each unit $v \in S$, let $y_{v, S} \in [0, 1]$ denote the continuous share of unit $v$ allocated to district support $S$.

The Master Problem is formulated as:
\begin{align}
    \min_{x, y} \quad & \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S}} \sum_{v \in S} \text{Cost}(v, S) \, y_{v, S} + \rho \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S}} \text{Diam}(S) \, x_S \label{eq:sup_obj} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S}} x_S = K \label{eq:sup_k} \\
    & \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S} : v \in S} y_{v, S} = 1 && \forall v \in V \label{eq:sup_cover} \\
    & \eta \, x_S \le y_{v, S} \le x_S && \forall S \in \mathcal{S}, v \in S \label{eq:sup_bound} \\
    & L_K \, x_S \le \sum_{v \in S} M_v \, y_{v, S} \le U_K \, x_S && \forall S \in \mathcal{S} \label{eq:sup_cap} \\
    & y_{v, S} \in \{0, 1\} && \forall v \in V_{\text{indivisible}}, S \in \mathcal{S} \label{eq:sup_int} \\
    & \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S} : \{u, v\} \subseteq S} x_S \ge 1 && \forall (u, v) \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{couple}} \label{eq:sup_couple} \\
    & x_S \in \{0, 1\}, \quad y_{v, S} \ge 0 && \forall S \in \mathcal{S}, v \in S \label{eq:sup_vars}
\end{align}
where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_k}) enforces the exact district count $K$.
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_cover}) enforces 100\% complete, non-overlapping coverage across all units, guaranteeing \textbf{zero unheld opportunity mass}.
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_bound}) guarantees that active contacts receive at least share $\eta = 0.05$ (preventing negligible slivers).
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_cap}) enforces hard capacity bounds $[L_K, U_K] = [\tau_K(1 - \epsilon), \tau_K(1 + \epsilon)]$ with $\epsilon \in [0.10, 0.12]$.
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_int}) enforces that smaller states ($V_{\text{indivisible}}$) remain intact as whole units without boundary splitting.
    \item Constraint~(\ref{eq:sup_couple}) enforces macro-regional pairing (such as CA1 and CA2 coupling restrictions).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Theoretical Guarantees of the Support Formulation}

\begin{theorem}[Contiguity and Conservation Guarantee]\label{thm:support_exact}
Every feasible solution $(x^*, y^*)$ to the Master Support Partitioning MILP (\ref{eq:sup_obj})--(\ref{eq:sup_vars}) induces a partition of $V$ into exactly $K$ districts satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{100\% Rook Contiguity:} Every activated district $S$ with $x_S^* = 1$ is an induced connected subgraph of $G$.
    \item \textbf{Zero Unheld Mass:} $\sum_{S : x_S^*=1} \sum_{v \in S} M_v y_{v, S}^* = \sum_{v \in V} M_v$ exactly, with $0.00\%$ unheld mass.
    \item \textbf{Zero Heuristic Relaxation Slack:} All district masses strictly satisfy $L_K \le M(S) \le U_K$ without post-hoc $\sigma$-tolerance expansions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:support}, the enumeration procedure admits only subsets $S$ whose induced subgraph $G[S]$ is connected. Because $x_S^* \in \{0, 1\}$ and $\sum_{S} x_S^* = K$, exactly $K$ subsets are activated, each inherently connected. Summing (\ref{eq:sup_cover}) over all $v \in V$ weighted by $M_v$ yields:
\[
\sum_{v \in V} M_v = \sum_{v \in V} M_v \sum_{S: v \in S} y_{v, S}^* = \sum_{S \in \mathcal{S}} \sum_{v \in S} M_v y_{v, S}^* = \sum_{S : x_S^*=1} M(S)
\]
Thus, total system mass is preserved exactly. Finally, constraint (\ref{eq:sup_cap}) binds the continuous variables directly, ensuring that no district can violate capacity bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Data Descaling and Scale Invariance}

\subsection{The Descaled Data Representation}
As implemented in \texttt{td/instance.py}, the raw instance data undergoes a canonical descaling transformation governed by $\kappa > 0$, defined as the median positive ZIP opportunity across the footprint:
\begin{equation}
    m_{\text{rel}}(z) = \frac{M_z}{\kappa}, \qquad s_i(z) = \frac{S_i(z)}{M_z}
\end{equation}
Upon loading, the pipeline reconstructs:
\begin{equation}
    M(z) = m_{\text{rel}}(z) = \frac{\text{real } M_z}{\kappa}, \qquad S_i(z) = s_i(z) \cdot m_{\text{rel}}(z) = \frac{\text{real } S_i(z)}{\kappa}
\end{equation}
Under this transformation, the national opportunity sum across CONUS is $W_0 \approx 3,268.41$ descaled units, and district target capacity becomes $\tau \approx 180\text{--}200$ descaled units (rather than $\approx \$10^9$ raw dollars).

\subsection{Proposition: Exact Scale Invariance of Nash Welfare}
\begin{proposition}[Scale Invariance of Nash Social Welfare]\label{prop:scale}
Let $\mathcal{I} = (G, (S_i)_{i=1}^n, S_{\text{free}}, M)$ be an instance. Scaling every currency field uniformly by $\kappa > 0$ yields instance $\kappa \mathcal{I}$. For every district assignment $w$ and any perimeter penalty weight $\rho \ge 0$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item $u_i^{\kappa \mathcal{I}}(z) = \kappa u_i^{\mathcal{I}}(z)$ and $g_{i,j}^{\kappa \mathcal{I}} = \kappa g_{i,j}^{\mathcal{I}}$.
    \item $\sum_{i=1}^n \log g_{i, \pi(i)}^{\kappa \mathcal{I}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \log g_{i, \pi(i)}^{\mathcal{I}} + n \log \kappa$.
    \item The additive shift $n \log \kappa$ is strictly independent of the allocation $w$ and assignment $\pi$. Consequently:
    \begin{equation}
    \arg\max_{w, \pi} \left( \sum_{i=1}^n \log g_{i, \pi(i)}^{\kappa \mathcal{I}} - \rho P(w) \right) \equiv \arg\max_{w, \pi} \left( \sum_{i=1}^n \log g_{i, \pi(i)}^{\mathcal{I}} - \rho P(w) \right)
    \end{equation}
    All objective differences, certificates, and optimality gaps are identical across $\mathcal{I}$ and $\kappa \mathcal{I}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Why Multiplicative Normalization Fails to Restore Curvature}
A natural question is whether normalizing the utility function by overall opportunity (either instance-wide $M_{\text{tot}}$ or district-level $M_j$) solves the flat-curvature issue of Nash welfare. Mathematically, it cannot:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Global Normalization ($g_{i,j} / M_{\text{tot}}$):} By Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}, dividing by a global constant simply subtracts $n \log M_{\text{tot}}$ from the objective. The argmax, gradients, and Hessian structure are completely untouched.
    \item \textbf{District Normalization ($g_{i,j} / M_j$):} Expanding $g_{i,j}$:
    \begin{equation}
    \frac{g_{i,j}}{M_j} = \underbrace{(c_1 - c_2) \frac{S_i(V_j)}{M_j}}_{\Delta_{i,j} / M_j} + \underbrace{\left[ c_2 \frac{T(V_j)}{M_j} + c_{\text{free}} \frac{S_{\text{free}}(V_j)}{M_j} + \lambda \right]}_{G_{0,j} / M_j}
    \end{equation}
    Dividing by $M_j$ scales numerator and denominator proportionally. The critical ratio:
    \begin{equation}
    \frac{\Delta_{i,j} / M_j}{G_{0,j} / M_j} = \frac{\Delta_{i,j}}{G_{0,j}}
    \end{equation}
    is strictly \textbf{dimensionless}. Because $G_{0,j} \gg \Delta_{i,j}$ (ambient district opportunity and cross-selling dwarf individual book differences), the Taylor expansion remains linear:
    \begin{equation}
    \log\left( \frac{G_{0,j}}{M_j} + \frac{\Delta_{i,j}}{M_j} \right) \approx \log\left(\frac{G_{0,j}}{M_j}\right) + \frac{\Delta_{i,j}}{G_{0,j}}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Multiplicative scaling cannot alter the dimensionless ratio $\Delta / G_0$. Only an \textbf{additive translation} ($g - d$) can shift the operating point of $\log(x)$ toward its steep curvature regime.

\section{The Disagreement Point Dilemma: Classical EF1 vs. Feasible Envy-Freeness}

\subsection{The Zero Threat Point and the Caragiannis Guarantee}
In classical cooperative bargaining theory (Nash, 1950), the Nash product is maximized over gains above a disagreement point $d = (d_1, \dots, d_n)$:
\begin{equation}
    \max_{\pi} \prod_{i=1}^n (u_i(\pi_i) - d_i)
\end{equation}
In \texttt{docs/MODEL\_FULL.md} and \texttt{docs/math\_note/math\_note.tex}, the baseline is fixed at $d = \mathbf{0}$. The foundational justification rests on the landmark theorem of Caragiannis et al.~(2019):
\begin{theorem}[Caragiannis et al., 2019]\label{thm:caragiannis}
For unconstrained fair division of indivisible goods under additive valuations and a zero disagreement point $d = \mathbf{0}$, any allocation maximizing the Nash Social Welfare $\prod_{i=1}^n u_i(A_i)$ is simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Pareto Optimal (PO)}, and
    \item \textbf{Envy-Free up to One Item (EF1)}: For any pair of agents $i, k$, there exists an item $z \in A_k$ such that:
    \begin{equation}
    u_i(A_i) \ge u_i(A_k \setminus \{z\})
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Why Shifting to \texorpdfstring{$d > 0$}{d > 0} Voids Classical EF1}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:caragiannis} relies directly on local exchange ratios: if agent $i$ envies agent $k$ by more than one good, shifting item $z \in A_k$ to $A_i$ strictly increases the Nash product:
\begin{equation}
    \left( 1 + \frac{u_i(z)}{u_i(A_i)} \right) \left( 1 - \frac{u_k(z)}{u_k(A_k)} \right) > 1
\end{equation}
When an additive disagreement vector $d > \mathbf{0}$ is introduced:
\begin{equation}
    \left( 1 + \frac{u_i(z)}{u_i(A_i) - d_i} \right) \left( 1 - \frac{u_k(z)}{u_k(A_k) - d_k} \right)
\end{equation}
If rep $i$ is driven near their reservation utility ($u_i - d_i \to \epsilon$), the marginal ratio $\frac{u_i(z)}{\epsilon}$ diverges to infinity. The optimizer responds by aggressively transferring goods from unconstrained agents to agent $i$, even when agent $i$ already possesses substantial bundle value. As established in the literature ledger (\texttt{docs/foundations/LIT\_economic-theory.md}, row~A4), subtracting a constant strictly voids the Caragiannis guarantee; no deterministic indivisible EF1 analogue exists for $d > 0$.

\subsection{The Reality: Capacity Bands Already Invalidate Classical EF1}
Crucially, \texttt{docs/foundations/DOMAIN\_economic-theory.md} (§2.1, §2.8) proves that the territory design problem \textbf{already violates the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:caragiannis}}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Quantity Capacity Bands:} Districts must satisfy $L \le M(V_j) \le U$. The optimizer cannot execute the arbitrary item transfers required by the Caragiannis exchange step without violating the capacity band.
    \item \textbf{Topological Contiguity:} Items (ZIPs) cannot be transferred if doing so disconnects the territory graph.
\end{enumerate}
Empirical measurement on the CONUS footprint demonstrates a representative utility spread of over $60\%$ (\texttt{MODEL\_U8-band}). Thus, classical unconstrained EF1 is already defunct on the ground.

\subsection{Transition to Feasible Envy-Freeness (FEFx)}
Under Generalized Assignment Constraints, Barman et al.~(2023) establish the appropriate axiomatic fairness concept:
\begin{definition}[Feasible Envy-Freeness up to Any Item (FEFx)]\label{def:fefx}
Let $\mathcal{F}_i$ denote the set of all feasible territories for representative $i$ (satisfying drivability from home base, spatial contiguity, and capacity bands $[L, U]$). An allocation $A$ is \textbf{Feasible Envy-Free up to Any Item (FEFx)} if for every pair of agents $i, k$:
\begin{equation}
    A_k \in \mathcal{F}_i \implies \forall z \in A_k, \; u_i(A_i) \ge u_i(A_k \setminus \{z\})
\end{equation}
If territory $A_k$ is not feasible for representative $i$ ($A_k \notin \mathcal{F}_i$), agent $i$ cannot claim legitimate envy against $A_k$.
\end{definition}
As succinctly formulated in the economics charter: \emph{``A wholesaler cannot envy a bundle they could not work.''} Under FEFx, cross-regional comparisons (e.g., an Ohio representative envying a Florida territory) are pruned by operational feasibility.

\subsection{Dropping Anonymity: Asymmetric Entitlements and Claims}
In sales force restructuring, representatives are not anonymous participants with equal claims to unowned goods. They hold \textbf{asymmetric historical entitlements} defined by their legacy books $S_i(Z)$. 

We formulate the staffing problem as an \textbf{asymmetric claims problem} (O'Neill, 1982; Thomson, 2003). The objective becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \max_{\pi} \sum_{i \in R} \log\left( g_{i, \pi(i)} - d_i \right)
\end{equation}
subject to the \textbf{Bargaining Set Non-Emptiness Guard}:
\begin{equation}
    d_i = \alpha_i S_i(Z) + d_{\text{floor}}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{floor}} = \gamma \min_j G_{0,j}$ ($\gamma \in [0.5, 0.8]$) ensures a common safety net, and $\alpha_i \in (0, 1)$ is determined via proportional claims bankruptcy scaling:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_i = \min\left( 1.0, \; \frac{M(\text{Region})}{\sum_{k \in \text{overlap}} S_k(\text{Region})} \right) \cdot (1 - \epsilon)
\end{equation}
This formulation achieves three vital properties:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Guaranteed Feasibility:} $g_{i,j} - d_i > 0$ for all valid district assignments.
    \item \textbf{Restored Curvature:} Denominators drop from $\approx 200$ to $\approx 10\text{--}20$, amplifying marginal resistance to book starvation by over $100\times$.
    \item \textbf{FEFx Compliance:} Envy is bounded with respect to operationally feasible assignments.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Planar Polygon Dissolution and Full Geographic Integrity}

A major downstream challenge in territorial optimization is translating discrete cell assignments into continuous, boundary-complete geometries for executive dashboards and geographic information systems (GIS).

\subsection{Eliminating the Swiss-Cheese Effect}
In naive GIS exports, plotting only commercial opportunity ZIP codes ($3,713$ populated ZIPs out of $33,791$ Census ZCTAs) creates vast white voids in rural areas. Furthermore, standard administrative boundaries fail when states like California or Texas are split across multiple districts.

To solve this, we implemented the \textbf{Planar Polygon Dissolution Pipeline} (see scripts \texttt{geom\_export.py} and \texttt{export\_tableau\_datasets.py} in \texttt{tools/}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Voronoi Proximity Tessellation:} Unpopulated and rural ZIP centroids are assigned to the nearest commercial district center in Lambert Azimuthal Equal Area (LAEA) projection.
    \item \textbf{State Boundary Clipping:} The dissolved cell polygons are strictly intersected with official US Census state boundary shapefiles.
    \item \textbf{WGS84 Transformation:} Cleaned geometries are transformed to standard coordinates (\texttt{EPSG:4326}) and exported to \texttt{tableau\_district\_reach.geojson} ($1,809$ district polygons across all 36 scenarios).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Master 33,791-ZCTA Crosswalk}
For relational database ingestion, we constructed the full nationwide crosswalk:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D} = 33,791 \text{ Census ZCTAs} \times 5 \text{ sub-channels} \times 36 \text{ scenarios} = 6,082,380 \text{ rows}
\end{equation}
compressed into \texttt{all\_scenarios\_nationwide\_zcta\_long.zip} ($27.2$~MB) under the 12-column canonical relational schema:
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{S}_{\text{schema}} = \big( & \texttt{scenario},\, \texttt{zip\_code},\, \texttt{current\_channel},\, \texttt{canonical\_channel}, \\
& \texttt{state},\, \texttt{model\_channel},\, \texttt{bundle},\, \texttt{district}, \\

\end{align*}
This bridges mathematical district definitions directly with ERP and CRM reporting without spatial omission.
