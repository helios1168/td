# P1: Fragmentation bounds (main line)
Question: In bin packing with item fragmentation (also "splittable item", "fragmentable item",
"bin packing with item fragmentation BPP-IF", "size-preserving fragmentation", "k-splittable"),
what lower bounds exist on the number of fragmented items, or on the number of fragments, when
bins have a fixed capacity window and the number of bins is free or bounded? In particular:
(a) structure results such as "an optimal solution fragments each item at most once" or "the
fragmentation graph is a forest" (kb has casazza2016 Thm 2.1 and kalcsics2005 §4.1 on this;
extend, don't repeat); (b) variants restricting fragments PER BIN (at most one fragment per bin,
our rule C) and what is proved about their feasibility or optimum; (c) necessary conditions for
feasibility of the kind "an item larger than U must be split; its fragments need room in bins
whose other items are whole", including any Hall-type or flow-based feasibility test on a
compatibility graph between items (our states only combine with neighbours: a bin may hold
fragments of item i only together with items adjacent to i); (d) LP and dual bounds that remain
valid when adjacency restricts which items may share a bin. Also check cutting-stock with
"pattern" vocabulary for the same bounds. Known names to check: Mandal-Chakrabarti-Ghose 1998,
Menakerman-Rom 2001, Epstein-van Stee, Casazza-Ceselli 2014/2016, Bertazzi et al., "bin packing
with conflicts" (the adjacency restriction) and its lower bounds (Gendreau-Laporte-Semet 2004,
Sadykov-Vanderbeck 2013).
