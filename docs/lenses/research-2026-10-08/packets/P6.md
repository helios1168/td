# P6: Necks as balanced minimum cuts; width-threshold connectivity (supporting)
Question: td's neck is a cut (A, rest) inside a district with edge weight (shared border length)
under 10 km whose smaller side holds >=5% of land area and is connected. What does the
literature offer for (a) checking it: minimum balanced cut / minimum quotient cut / edge
expansion with a side-size floor, exact algorithms and complexity for small planar graphs, and
whether the connectivity of the small side changes the complexity; (b) imposing it inside a
partition model: "k-edge-connected districts", weighted edge-connectivity constraints, "contiguity
with minimum connection width", and cut-row separation analogous to the contiguity cut rows
(kb validi2022 Thm 1, Prop. 2); (c) proxies: cut-edge compactness (kb validibuchanan2022),
Polsby-Popper's neck sensitivity (kb duchin2018), isoperimetric measures, and any paper that
relates a compactness score to a bottleneck bound. Also look outside districting: survivable
network design, "well-connected" or "robust" clusters, land-use zoning with minimum corridor
width, and the bottleneck/cheeger literature on planar graphs. Deliver: the right name for our
check, the cheapest exact algorithm for it, and whether anyone has put a width-threshold cut
condition into an exact partition model.
