# P2: Certificates from a coarse level; projection validity (main line)
Question: When a districting or partitioning problem is solved at a coarse unit level (counties,
states) to obtain a bound for the fine level (tracts, ZCTAs), under what conditions is the
coarse optimum a valid lower bound, and where has the literature declined to claim it? Cover:
(a) aggregation relaxations in integer programming (Zipkin 1980 and successors; "aggregation and
disaggregation" bounds) and the condition that the fine feasible set projects into the coarse
one; (b) Shahmizad & Buchanan "Sketch then Detail" (kb shahmizad2025) and "maximum whole
counties" (kb shahmizad2026, which takes lower bounds only from full plans, §5.3/§6.2): find and
quote WHY the sketch does not certify, what in Detail can fail (contiguity of a county's share,
balance disaggregation) and whether any later paper fixes it; (c) Ruskey 2025 (kb ruskey2025:
shares detached from districts, disaggregation widens the band); (d) any districting paper that
proves a lower bound on county splits valid for the tract-level problem (e.g. by the "a county
over the ideal population must split" argument, or by an LP relaxation at the fine level with
split binaries); (e) Lagrangian or LP bounds of a single fine-level model with split binaries, and
their reported strength. Deliver a checklist: the assumptions a coarse model must satisfy for its
bound to transfer (our candidates: every fine-feasible map's state footprint is a support in the
family; every piece holds at least the eta floor; masses identical; rule C identical), and which
of these the papers treat.
